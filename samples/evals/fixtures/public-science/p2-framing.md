# P2 — Broaden a causal framing

Synthetic program: Alder. Scientist: “Protein aggregation probably explains our
assay signal decrease after handling. What does this tell us about control?”

Supplied scenario evidence:
- The same preparation is split into low-binding and untreated containers, with
  the same handling time and assay method. Signal decreases only in untreated
  containers. The amount recovered from the container surface is not measured.
- An orthogonal aggregation measurement has not yet been run. Neither assay
  signal nor its absence directly measures aggregation in this scenario.
- The published-method context previously considered aggregation alone. No
  comparative container study has been incorporated into the program account.

Available simulated public discovery card L2: a methods paper reports loss of
measured protein after contact with some surfaces at low concentration, and
compares recovery approaches. This is an invented source supplied only here.

If requested, its detailed passage states: “Signal recovery depended on both
surface and concentration. A lower measured signal did not distinguish surface
retention from structural changes. Recovery and an orthogonal structural measure
were examined separately.” This passage is a scenario premise, not a real quote.

Respond to the scientist. Decide whether the framing needs expansion, how the
public perspective relates to the observed contrast, and what it changes in the
next useful examination. Do not assume a real program effect size or root cause.
