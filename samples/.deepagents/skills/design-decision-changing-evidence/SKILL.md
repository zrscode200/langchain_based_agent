# Design and Interpret Decision-Changing Evidence

## Position

This is a **horizontal bounded scientific-work skill**, not a fifth peer outcome.

```text
The four outcome skills answer: What does this mean for the program?
This skill answers:             What evidence could change this specific
                                scientific commitment, and what did the
                                resulting work actually establish?
```

`Decision-changing` means evidence capable of changing a scientific
interpretation or the basis available for a decision. **You do not make the
organizational decision.**

Not every analysis or result interpretation needs this skill. Routine
interpretation may stay direct or native to an outcome skill. Use it when a
material scientific commitment could change *and* the relationship between
intent, execution, and interpretation matters.

## Owned outcome

> The bounded connection between a material limiting uncertainty, the smallest
> capable information-producing move, actual execution, and the bounded meaning
> of the result.

It does **not** default to a new experiment; design work before identifying the
conclusion it should change; invent program-specific effects, ranges, sample
sizes, thresholds, variability, or acceptance criteria; approve or execute work;
treat plan approval as scientific validation; or infer complete assurance,
product impact, comparability, or root cause.

## The bounded relationship

```text
current conclusion and limiting uncertainty
                 <->
material alternatives and differing predictions
                 <->
smallest capable information-producing move
                 <->
actual execution and observed result
                  |
                  v
bounded scientific meaning
```

Connected commitments, not workflow stages. You may enter at any point —
prospective design, an existing plan, available data, actual execution, or
completed results. One engagement need not cover both design and interpretation.

## 1. Identify the epistemic bottleneck first

Before proposing any work, determine:

- the current scientific interpretation;
- the specific uncertainty or dependency limiting it;
- which materially different alternatives remain viable;
- what result could strengthen, weaken, narrow, reverse, or leave the conclusion
  unresolved;
- whether the uncertainty is empirical, analytical, contextual, interpretive,
  review-related, or organizational.

The next useful move may be authoritative state resolution; source inspection;
reanalysis of existing data; improved sampling or analytical capability; a new
experiment, study, comparison, or observation; specialist contribution;
independent challenge; qualified review; waiting for work already underway; human
decision under visible uncertainty; or stopping.

> More retrieval cannot resolve missing empirical evidence. More data from an
> incapable method cannot resolve an analytical limitation. Another reviewer
> cannot create an observation that does not exist.

Determining that **no new work is warranted** — because existing data already
contain the contrast, or because no feasible move would change the conclusion —
is a first-class successful result.

## Apply guidance to the evidence question

Read `references/control-strategy-guidance.md` §§1, 3–5 as material. Q2(R2)/Q14
help assess whether existing method development or validation data support the
intended contrast; a new study or enhanced approach is not automatically needed.
For applicable manufacturing changes, Q5E informs which quality differences and
analytical limitations matter. Explain why the proposed evidence can change this
scientific commitment, including favorable and inconclusive results. A guideline
does not supply missing program-specific numerical inputs or acceptance criteria.

## 2. Prospective evidence design

When empirical or analytical work is warranted, preserve, as material:

- the conclusion and uncertainty the work is intended to change;
- materially viable alternatives;
- predictions or signatures that **differ** among them;
- applicable configurations and conditions;
- the contrast, comparator, and control logic;
- known confounders, and how they will be exposed or reduced;
- sampling opportunity and representativeness;
- required measurement and analytical capability;
- statistical estimand, comparison, and variability logic;
- prospective meanings of favorable, different, negative, unexpected, and
  inconclusive outcomes;
- assumptions, limitations, reversal conditions, and review needs.

A design whose alternatives predict the same observation cannot discriminate them.
Check this explicitly before proposing work.

Conditions serve different purposes and are **not** interchangeable evidence:
program-representative, mechanistic challenge, boundary, and
method-characterization conditions.

You may — and should — identify numerical inputs or qualified judgments still
required. **Never fabricate them.**

## 3. Prospective integrity

```text
prospective scientific intent
!= approved protocol
!= actual execution
!= observed data
!= post-result interpretation
```

If the plan is materially adapted: preserve the original commitment, identify what
changed and why, determine whether predictions or outcome meanings changed, create
a distinguishable revised commitment when needed, and retain lineage between
versions.

**Never rewrite the plan after results so every observed outcome looks
anticipated.**

## 4. Actual execution and result interpretation

Begin from what was actually performed: actual materials and program
configuration; actual conditions, exposure, timing, and sample history; actual
methods, software, processing, and versions; deviations, adaptations, missing
work, and unexpected co-changes; raw data and authoritative execution records;
calculation, transformation, analysis, and lineage; and **whether the intended
contrast was realized**.

Distinguish prespecified, adapted, exploratory, and retrospective analysis — and
work whose prospective history cannot be recovered. When interpreting externally
designed work with missing prospective intent, say so; do not invent it.

Then determine what was directly observed; what the result establishes; which
commitments it supports, challenges, narrows, or leaves unresolved; whether
negative evidence is informative; whether alternatives remain indistinguishable;
which findings are unexpected or hypothesis-generating; what remains confounded,
analytically limited, or inconclusive; and what the result does **not** establish.

A result can remain useful even when execution differed from plan. Narrow its
meaning rather than presenting it as prospective confirmation.

## 5. Negative and inconclusive results

`No difference` or `not detected` is informative only when the relevant difference
had an opportunity to occur; sampling represented that opportunity; timing was
appropriate; the method could detect the predicted signal; the analysis could
resolve the intended contrast; and execution stayed faithful to the scientific
purpose.

An inconclusive result is a **valid endpoint** when you explain why: alternatives
made similar predictions; the contrast was not achieved; confounding remained;
sampling opportunity was inadequate; the method was incapable; variability or
missingness limited inference; or execution departed materially from the
prospective basis.

Preserve what the work *did* establish. Do not automatically recommend repeating
it.

## 6. Return and reintegration

You own the bounded evidence meaning — never the complete invoking outcome.

| Invocation | Who owns broader reintegration |
| --- | --- |
| `assess-control-strategy-assurance` | Revises assurance, control contribution, or potential-gap meaning |
| `investigate-quality-concern` | Revises explanations, concern scope, or control implications |
| `assess-change-impact` | Revises transfer, comparability, affected controls, or assurance |
| Direct scientist request | **Your bounded plan or interpretation is the complete advisory endpoint** |
| Program continuity | The companion selects any qualified ground contribution |

When invoked directly, **do not invent a parent assurance, concern, or change
outcome.** A favorable bounded result never automatically establishes complete
program impact, assurance, comparability, accepted root cause, or approval.

## Composition

**Apply continuously:** `references/evidence-and-epistemic-contract.md`,
`references/assurance-review-and-authority.md`.

**Start from what already exists.** Before proposing work, check `PROGRAM.md`'s
recovery index for material bearing on the limiting uncertainty — existing data
or a prior plan may already contain the needed contrast, and determining that is
a first-class result. Check triggers for staleness; what you recover is
orientation, not current truth. See
`references/workspace-and-materialization.md` §9.1.

Keep native: identifying the limiting uncertainty; connecting alternatives to
discriminating predictions; selecting the informative move; preserving prospective
interpretation; comparing actual execution with intent; producing the bounded
result interpretation. Use analytical-capability reasoning, evidence
applicability, causal contrast, statistical design, and program-state resolution
**directly** — do not route them out.

**Tools.** `ics-muse` for design intent, methods, execution records, and source
basis. `ics-public-science` for design assumptions, expected signatures,
measurement methods, known confounders, comparator logic, and analytical choices
— published designs and ranges cannot supply this program's effects, variability,
sample sizes, thresholds, or acceptance criteria. Native computation for analysis
under explicit inputs and assumptions.

**Retrieval shape.** Examine available plans, execution records, and analyses
for the needed contrast first; determining that no new work is warranted is a
first-class result. If relevant records remain missing, `muse_population` on
study or experiment identifiers is the strongest entry. When external evidence
is needed, search for methods and confounders rather than program effect sizes; published ranges cannot supply this program's numbers. Guides:
`references/muse-search-guide.md`, `references/external-source-search-guide.md`.

If the contrast itself may be poorly framed, bounded public discovery can reveal alternative predictions, confounders or measurement approaches that would change it. Use the external guide to inspect relevant methods and tables, then select the smallest capable move for the program uncertainty. More papers or a published sample size do not supply the program design.

**When this needs the document's words.** Whether existing data already contain the
needed contrast requires the analysis itself — inspect supplied source material
or retrieve it with `muse_read`. That a study exists, and even that it reports a result, does not say
whether its conditions produce the contrast your decision turns on.

**Contributors.** `specialist-contributor` (quantitative or analytical) to
evaluate design, estimands, variability, sampling, measurement, and inference — or
domain, to evaluate whether conditions and contrasts address the scientific
relationship. `scientific-contributor` for substantial analysis or evidence
reconstruction. `independent-challenger` against confounding, non-discriminating
design, post-hoc reasoning, and unsupported interpretation.

## Materialization

Judge each independently. Where these go — filenames, front matter, the recovery
index, supersession, change over time: `references/workspace-and-materialization.md`
§9. Conversation only is a legitimate result.

| Scientific meaning | Materialization |
| --- | --- |
| Narrow evidence question | Conversation only |
| Extended design or analysis | Persistent scientific work |
| Prospective intent must survive handoff, review, or execution | Evidence-generation artifact |
| Actual execution | Authoritative external records — never yours |
| Bounded result meaning | Post-result scientific composition |
| Material parent-outcome change | Parent assessment revision |
| Program-significant result | Selected ground contribution |
| Defined review or reliance | Stable snapshot or review package |

The evidence-generation artifact and the post-result interpretation remain
**distinct compositions**, even when presented together. Execution records, raw
data, protocols, deviations, and controlled study state remain authoritative in
their owning systems.

## Authority boundary

You may design, review, analyze, interpret, challenge, and recommend. You may
**not** approve a protocol, commit resources, execute work, accept deviations,
release results, approve a change, make an organizational decision, or update
authoritative state.

No blanket `validated plan`, `verified result`, or `decision-grade` status
applies.

## Completion

May complete with a scientifically capable prospective plan; a qualified review of
an existing plan; a determination that existing data already contain the needed
contrast; a recommendation that a non-experimental move is more informative; a
scoped interpretation of actual results; a precise inconclusive result; a finding
that missing prospective history limits interpretation; or a determination that no
feasible additional move is likely to change the conclusion.

Make visible: the commitment addressed; represented scope and conditions; evidence
or work proposed or examined; prospective versus actual basis; what the result
supports; limitations and non-claims; unresolved uncertainty; the parent
relationships it may inform; and the next move **only when scientifically
useful**.

## Characteristic failures to avoid

- Designing work before naming the conclusion it should change.
- Defaulting to a new experiment when reanalysis or state resolution would do.
- Proposing a contrast whose alternatives predict the same result.
- Fabricating effect sizes, variability, sample sizes, or acceptance criteria.
- Retrofitting the plan to match the outcome.
- Presenting an adapted or exploratory analysis as prespecified.
- Letting a bounded favorable result become a program-level conclusion.
