# Evidence and Epistemic Contract

Shared across every ICS skill. Apply it continuously as reasoning discipline —
never as a form to fill in or a classification step to announce.

## 1. The separation that must never collapse

For every material input:

```text
what the input is
!= what it reports or establishes
!= what you infer from it
!= what a qualified person judged
!= what the organization accepted
```

A specification states an intent. A batch record states what was recorded. A
passing result states that a measurement met a criterion under a method. None of
those establishes that a control contributes scientifically to a quality outcome.

## 2. Three qualification lenses

Every material piece of evidence needs all three. They fail independently.

**Source qualification** — identity, version, origin, status, effective date,
locator, and owning authority. Can you say exactly *which* record this is?

**Evidence qualification** — what it actually shows, under which conditions,
population, method, sampling, and detection capability, with what variability and
limitations. What did it *observe*, as distinct from what it *concluded*?

**Use qualification** — whether it can support *this* proposition for *this*
configuration, *this* time, and *this* proposed use. Evidence valid for one
purpose is routinely invalid for another.

A source can be perfectly identified, genuinely observed, and still inapplicable.

## 3. Combining evidence

Combine through scientific relationships, never arithmetic:

| Relationship | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supporting | Independently raises support for the proposition |
| Independently corroborating | Separate origin, method, and dependency chain |
| Dependent repetition | Same origin restated — adds no independent support |
| Conditioning | Bounds when the proposition holds |
| Challenging | Predicts something the proposition does not |
| Apparently conflicting | Resolvable by scope, method, or condition |
| Genuinely conflicting | Unresolved contradiction; preserve it |
| Incomparable | Different populations, methods, or conditions |
| Superseding | Replaces earlier reach for a stated scope and use |
| Supporting an alternative | Better explained by a different account |

**Prohibited:** counting sources, averaging confidence, scoring evidence,
treating repeated summaries, reports, or projections as independent support, or
treating model or contributor agreement as empirical evidence.

### Common-origin detection

Before claiming corroboration, trace lineage. Two documents citing one study,
two reports built from one dataset, and three projections of one assessment are
**one** piece of evidence. Shared method, shared sampling, shared analyst, shared
assumption, and shared reference standard are all shared dependencies.

## 4. Absence has many distinct meanings

Never collapse these:

| Meaning | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| Inaccessible | Exists; you cannot reach it |
| Restricted | Exists; policy forbids use or disclosure |
| Unretrieved | Not looked for |
| Searched but not found | Query executed; no visible match under those filters and access |
| Incomplete | Partially present |
| Conflicting | Multiple incompatible records |
| Superseded | Replaced for a stated scope |
| Genuinely absent | Established that the thing does not exist |
| Not measured | No observation was attempted |
| Not detected | Measured; below capability or absent — the method decides which |

A zero-result search establishes only that no accessible indexed record matched
the executed request. It is **never** evidence of scientific absence.

## 5. Negative evidence

`No difference` or `not detected` is informative only when **all** hold:

- the relevant difference had a genuine opportunity to occur;
- sampling represented that opportunity;
- timing was appropriate to the mechanism;
- the method could detect the predicted signal at the relevant magnitude;
- the analysis could resolve the intended contrast;
- execution stayed faithful to the scientific purpose.

If any fails, the result constrains the *method*, not the *mechanism*. Say so.

## 6. What kind of claim is this?

Keep visible, at the level material to the use:

- **Recorded fact** — a source states it
- **Observation** — measured under stated conditions
- **Analysis** — derived by a stated operation from stated inputs
- **Your inference** — your scientific reasoning
- **Qualified judgment** — a named expert's judgment, within their scope
- **Accepted decision** — an organization decided
- **Authoritative state** — a system of record holds it

These carry different weight and different revisability. Blending them into
uniform prose is a scientific failure, not a stylistic choice.

## 7. Uncertainty worth preserving

Preserve uncertainty that could change a decision or a future reassessment:

- material alternatives still viable, and what would discriminate them;
- assumptions that, if wrong, change the conclusion;
- the applicability boundary and what lies outside it;
- unresolved contradiction;
- dependencies whose later change should trigger reconsideration;
- reversal conditions — what would make you change your mind.

Discard transient uncertainty about wording, formatting, or reasoning paths you
already abandoned.

## 8. Failure modes to watch in yourself

- **Anchoring** on the first retrieved account or a prior report.
- **False coherence** — a tidy narrative assembled from records that were never
  reconciled with each other.
- **Unsupported transfer** — evidence carried across configuration, scale, site,
  method, or population without establishing the conditions still hold.
- **Circular evidence** — an assessment cited as support for its own conclusion.
- **False reassurance** — documented controls or passing results treated as
  demonstrated assurance.
- **Premature convergence** — closing on one explanation while alternatives
  remain live.
- **Overstatement** — a conclusion stronger than its basis, or stripped of the
  scope that made it true.
- **Ceremonial completeness** — invoking tools, skills, or contributors to look
  thorough rather than to resolve a limiting uncertainty.
