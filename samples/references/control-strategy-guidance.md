# Applying ICH guidance to control-strategy consultation

This is a maintained scientific synthesis for the companion, not official
guideline text. Read the applicable parts when establishing or examining a
control strategy, comparing control options, assessing change, or evaluating the
evidence needed for those questions. Use the [source index](ich-source-index.md)
for official editions and section locators.

Contents: §1 applicability and claim type; §2 controls as a system;
§3 priorities and alternatives; §4 analytical evidence; §5 manufacturing change;
§6 lifecycle and viral safety; §7 retrieval, contributors, and persistence.

## 1. Establish applicability and what kind of statement you are making

Resolve only dimensions that can change the reasoning: product/modality, drug
substance or drug product, configuration, development/lifecycle stage, question,
and jurisdiction when a regulatory implication is being considered. State a
material unknown and reason conditionally rather than completing an intake form.

Distinguish:

- **Guideline principle:** identified ICH edition and section, with its scope.
- **Regional guidance or requirement:** identify the implementing authority and
  the actual basis; ICH adoption alone does not establish a universal legal duty.
  FDA guidance describes recommendations unless it cites a binding requirement.
- **Program commitment:** what the applicable controlled record actually commits
  this program to; a guideline does not establish that record.
- **Scientific assessment or advice:** your application of a principle to the
  program evidence, with assumptions, alternatives, and limits.

Absence of a particular document, enhanced approach, or label is not by itself a
scientific deficiency. A relevant commitment can nevertheless remain binding on
the program even where another scientific approach could be reasonable.

## 2. Explain and examine the collection of controls

**Source basis:** Q8(R2) Annex §§2.1–2.3, 2.5; Q11 §§6.1–6.2;
Q6B §§2.1, 2.5, 3–4.

Connect the quality objective and attributes to plausible variability or harm
pathways, the contribution of each control, the rationale for criteria, and the
evidence of implementation and performance. Ask what the collection achieves and
where its elements depend on the same assumption, measurement, or process step.

For drug substance, Q11 allows control of attributes upstream when justified;
the specification is one component of the strategy. Q6B distinguishes
characterization from the justified subset of tests used for specifications.
Examine the relevant upstream evidence before calling an omitted release test
a gap. Conversely, a test's presence and passing results do not establish that
the overall pathway is adequately controlled.

Q8 permits different development approaches. Do not demand a design space or
real-time release testing merely because an enhanced approach is described
(Appendix 1). Explain the rationale for the approach actually used.

**Worked application — invented, qualitative illustration:** A drug-substance
impurity is not tested on every release lot. A characterization account identifies
its origin, removal studies cover the represented input challenge and operating
range, and executed records support the relevant process conditions. Explain the
removal contribution and its conditions. If a new input source exceeds the studied
challenge, requalify that relationship; neither an automatic release-test demand
nor unchanged reliance on the earlier account follows from the guideline alone.
This illustration supplies no program fact, limit, or approved control change.

## 3. Advise on priorities and compare alternatives

**Source basis:** Q9(R1) §§3, 4.3–4.6, 5.1–5.3.

Q9 links risk reasoning to scientific knowledge and patient protection and calls
for rigor proportionate to uncertainty, importance, and complexity. It allows
qualitative and quantitative methods; it does not supply this program's scores.

When advice is requested, compare material issues by their relationship to
intended quality, plausible failure pathways, existing protection, uncertainty,
and the information likely to change the assessment. Explain why one deserves
attention before another. Counts of missing documents or weak connections are
not estimates of severity or probability.

For candidate controls, use a common quality proposition. Compare prevention,
reduction, detection/interception, conditions of effectiveness, analytical
capability, dependencies, residual uncertainty, and relevant feasibility
constraints supplied by the team. A control may introduce other vulnerabilities.
Recommend a conditional preference where supported and identify what would
reverse it. If criteria or evidence cannot discriminate options, state what
would; do not invent a ranking.

Scientific risk analysis and advisory priorities are allowed. Numerical analysis
needs explicit inputs, definitions/scales, assumptions, and rationale. Keep it
distinct from evidence-confidence scoring and from an organization's risk
acceptance or authorized action.

## 4. Establish what analytical evidence can support

**Source basis:** Q2(R2) §§1.2, 2–3; Q14 §§1.2, 2–6.

Q2 ties validation to intended use and appropriate performance characteristics.
Q14 permits minimal and enhanced development approaches and use of relevant
prior knowledge; development data may support validation without unnecessary
repetition.

For the inference at hand, examine the attribute, method/version, sample matrix,
range, specificity/selectivity, accuracy, precision, sampling, and relevant
robustness. Select the characteristics material to the question. A validation
label for one use does not resolve capability for another.

Before recommending new work, determine whether existing qualified data already
contain the needed contrast. If methods, sample handling, or processing changed,
ask what signatures distinguish a measurement effect from a product/process
effect. Preserve what an inconclusive result establishes at a narrower scope.
Do not demand an enhanced approach or analytical target profile solely as a label.

## 5. Assess manufacturing changes and comparability

**Source basis:** Q5E §§1.3–1.4, 2.1–2.4; Q12 §§1.2, 2–3, 6 when applicable.

Within Q5E's scope, comparability does not require identical quality attributes.
Evaluate observed differences using relevant knowledge and evidence concerning
quality, safety, and efficacy. Similar measured results are limited by the
methods' ability to detect relevant differences. Select evidence based on the
actual change, including characterization, process, specification, and stability
relationships where material.

State a supported, conditional, partitioned, or unsupported **scientific
proposition** with its exact scope. An analytical comparison can support part of
the case without establishing the complete comparability conclusion. Neither a
scientific recommendation nor guideline alignment authorizes implementation.

For a postapproval regulatory question, Q12's established conditions must be
distinguished from supporting information. Resolve actual registered commitments
and regional implementation; do not assign reporting categories from general
scientific risk reasoning alone.

## 6. Use lifecycle and topic guidance when the question needs it

**Source basis:** Q10 §§1.6, 3.2.1, 3.2.3; Q11 §9; Q5A(R2) §§1–6.

Use lifecycle knowledge and actual performance or change information to revisit
affected control relationships. A stable historical account does not make a new
configuration supported. Q10 informs the scientific use of knowledge; it does
not specify the companion's storage architecture.

For viral-safety questions within Q5A(R2)'s scope, examine the contributions and
limitations of cell-substrate qualification, appropriate testing, and process
clearance. Read the applicable product and method provisions before proposing
specific study expectations. Do not infer complete viral assurance from one
negative test or import a generic numerical clearance target.

## 7. Retrieval, contributor use, and saved meaning

- Start from available qualified evidence and applicable guidance. Search only
  for unresolved information that could change the answer. Missing empirical
  evidence cannot be supplied by another guideline citation.
- For guideline text, use an identified local official copy or available access
  to the official ICH/authority document. The PubMed/Europe PMC connector retrieves
  scientific literature; it is not an official-guideline full-text service.
  A commentary article cannot silently stand in for the guideline.
- External queries use public concepts or public guideline identifiers only;
  existing disclosure restrictions still apply. If official access is unavailable,
  identify which inference needs verification and continue unaffected reasoning.
- Contributor briefs include relevant guidance/sections and applicability
  assumptions only when material. Standalone contributors read this reference as
  needed. Independent reconstructors receive source principles and evidence,
  without the parent's preferred conclusion or program-specific application.
- When guidance materially informs an answer, identify the source section and
  connect it to the program evidence and your inference. Do not recite a reading
  list. For saved meaning, include the edition and application rationale in the
  existing basis, with revision/applicability changes as triggers where relevant.
  No new persistent object, required artifact, or autonomous process is implied.
